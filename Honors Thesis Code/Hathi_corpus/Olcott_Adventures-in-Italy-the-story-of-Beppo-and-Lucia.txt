PREFACE
to the WORLD'S CHILDREN Series
EACH YEAR more people of the Western World set out
by ship and plane to visit the countries of the Old World.
Many of them are boys and girls, eager to see how their
Dutch, French, Italian, Swiss, Norwegian and Swedish cous-
ins live. But even if there were ten times as many ships and
planes available as there are now, only a relatively small
number of young Americans would be able to explore these
fascinating lands where Jean and Fanchon, Klaas and Jansje,
Beppo and Lucia, Olaf and Ane, Erik and Britta, or Anton
and Trini live.
Most American boys and girls still will have to be con-
tent to read about those countries they cannot visit and the
people who live in them. Many books already have been
written about them, but very few of those books make their
young readers feel so happily at home or so intimately fa-
miliar with the customs and daily living, the legends and all
the memorable sights and scenes to be found in each of these
faraway lands.
The author, Virginia Olcott, has lived in each one of the
countries about which she has written. She knows hundreds
of boys and girls in each of these countries, and she knows
the games they play, the clothes they wear, the homes in the
cities and villages where they live, and all the colorful
legends and lore of each country.
Because the author's research is accurate and up-to-date,
the consulates of the various countries have given these
books their enthusiastic approval, and millions of young
readers, we feel sure, will do the same.

FOREWORD
DEAR BOYS AND GIRLS:
Come, spend a year with Beppo and Lucia in sun-
bright Italy. You will float on a silvery canal in a gleam-
ing gondola. You may help Beppo and Lucia keep
Christmas on Old Befana's Day and try to catch jump-
ing crickets on the Cricket Festival.
Then there are Wooden Long Nose, Fata Dianora,
Rocco and his roasted chestnuts, and many beautiful
things of pink marble, of gilded leather, and of coral
and gold and silver.
Come, listen to the exciting stories of the Fire Moun-
tain, of the City on Seven Hills, and of big-mouthed
Bigi, and learn the funny little rhymes of Italy.
But the very best of all, the jolliest time of all, you
will have with Beppo and Lucia on Uncle Gino's farm.
There are little fat Como, the cream-colored oxen, the
noisy donkey, Toto, and olives and vineyards, and silk-
worms in their golden houses.
Come, come, boys and girls of America! Come, all
you World's Children! Beppo and Lucia are calling you
to their sunny Italy.

"Listen, Beppo!" cried Lucia. She stopped pulling
candies out of her stocking. She held up her finger.
"Listen to your bells of Florence! They are singing!"
Ring, ring, ring, ring, ring, ring! Music fell from
towers and church tops. So many Italian bells were
ringing that Lucia could not count them. Big bells and
baby bells were calling with voices loud and deep, or
silver clear. They were flinging sweet notes over roofs
and steeples, over shops and houses of the city of Flor-
ence. They were sending them straight into Beppo's
playroom.
Beppo knelt on the rug. He was busy pulling out
candies from his own stocking. He was tired of those
bells. Night and day, ever since he was born, they had
filled his playroom with their voices. He thought that
his little friend Lucia was foolish.

"Ecco!" Her mother nodded at the new doll. "That's

"It's round and fat like little Como at the farm," she

BEPPO'S OWN FLORENCE
Yes, Lucia knew that Beppo's city was wonderful.
Beppo Rossi's lovely city of Florence lay in a wide,
sunny valley in Italy. All around it were mountains cov-
ered with pines, oaks, chestnuts, and tall, pointed cypress
trees. On the lower hills nestled white farmhouses among
olive groves and vineyards. Along the roads, from be-
hind high garden walls peeped the tops of villas. At
Maytime, roses and purple wistaria trailed over the
walls. In the villas lived the rich folk. In the white farm-
houses lived the farmers with their families.
10
Beppo's city was called the "City of Flowers" be-
cause once, long ago-hundreds of years ago-the valley
was covered with beautiful lilies and other lovely blos-
soms. So the people who built Florence in that valley
named her after the flowers. And they took for the
emblem of their city the beautiful Florentine lily.
But now the flowers were gone. In the place where
they used to grow, Beppo saw, when he went out, paved
streets hundreds of years old. Some of them were nar-
row and dusky. Shops, ancient palaces, and houses of
roughly cut stone lined the dim ways. But on the broad
new streets were fine apartment houses and hotels and
little parks gay with trees and statues.
