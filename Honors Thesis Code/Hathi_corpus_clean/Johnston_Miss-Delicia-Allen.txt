MR. HORATIO Annesley arrived in September. He
proved a gentleman long and lank and dry and sad,
when he was not quite eerily gay. He was not a gentle-
man whom you could like all at once - every bit of
him - but undeniably he grew on Indian Leap. Back

It was a learning that quaintly combined to command
respect and to be taken humorously. Mr. Horatio An-
nesley himself seemed to regard it so, for all that it

Immediately after this or that necessary promulga-

A man emerged from the orchard, keeping the path
that crossed the dell. She observed the distant figure
idly, reckoning that it might be Henry Bench. A silverslip of a stream, crossed by a log, wound through the
dell. Now the man was on this side. It was not Henry
Bench. Some one - she had thought of Mr. Peveril
Brown - had come in at the great gate a while ago. It
was not Mr. Peveril Brown. Henry Bench was short,
Mr. Brown stooped and withered.
A path ran down the dell toward the overseer's house
and a path came this way. The man, stopping, looked
about him. Apparently he caught the golden, sun-
lighted hilltop and the ash tree and Delicia, standing
forth from its shade, for he began rapidly to come
toward her.
And it was Murray!
